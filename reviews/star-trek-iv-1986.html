Review: Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home (1986)

'Admiral, there be whales here!'

★★★★

Leonard Nimoy and William Shatner as Spock and Captain Kirk.

Yes. Yes. This is the infamous "Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home," a film that combines humor, heart, and a message about environmental conservation. Also known as, "The Whale Movie."

This Star Trek film is the funniest, heartwarming, and most environmentally conscious of the original series. This is Leonard Nimoy's second and last Star Trek film he directed, and it showcases his unique blend of storytelling and direction. We see how much he's grown as a director since "The Search for Spock."

Leonard Nimoy's direction shines through in every scene, balancing the sci-fi elements with genuine emotion and humor. He was able to pull a performance out of everyone and create a safe and fun environment on set. He's worked with the main cast since mid '60s, so, there was no need for him to do any homework.

The biggest takeaway is the film's plot. It's about retrieving the humpback whales from the past to save the future. This shouldn't work at all, but it does. It's very absurd on paper, but Nimoy made it work.

Each member of the Enterprise gets something to do in this adventure, contributing to the film's charm and depth. They're all highlights, and bring out many laughs. I believe Spock gets the most laughs. At least from me. I mean, we see Spock goes in dressed up as a Sensai, knocks out an annoying stereotypical punkass prick on the bus, mind melts with a whale, uses "colorful metaphors," plus sarcasm, and smiles at the end. It doesn't get any better than that!

I'm sure when the script was completed, everyone had the biggest grins on their faces. Like I said, it's a very absurd script, but it works wonderfully on screen. Seeing the crew trying to fit into the 20th century is comedic gold.

I liked the character, Dr. Gillian Taylor (Catherine Hicks) quite a lot. She added a nice touch of humanity and warmth to the story. Already, she suspects that Kirk and Spock, especially Spock, are not telling the whole truth and are up to something. So, to try to ease her tension and suspicion, Kirk invites her to dinner. She asks if they enjoy Italian food, and Kirk and Spock perform a beautiful verbal ballet predicated on the recurring joke that Spock, as a Vulcan, cannot tell a lie. Find another space opera where verbal contrast adds humor.

While a few don't appreciate this film as much as others in the series, I find it to be a delightful blend of humor, heart, and a timely message about conservation. I would happily rank it as my second favorite, behind "Wrath of Khan."

Rating: 4/4 stars.
