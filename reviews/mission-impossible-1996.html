Review: Mission: Impossible (1996)

★★★

Tom Cruise as Ethan Hunt in Mission Impossible.

I'll admit, I'm not big on these kinds of big-budget action movies; they're too loud, over the top, and often lack depth. No sense of stakes or character development really draws me in. Even the plot is nothing special. It's just an excuse to get something blowing up on screen. Explosions are cool and all, but where's the plot?

I had little interest in the "Mission: Impossible" series until recently. My curiosity about the hype surrounding it and the director, Brian De Palma, were the main reasons I decided to give it a try. I have always been fascinated by De Palma and have enjoyed most of his work. Whether it's "Carrie," "Blow Out," "Body Double," "Scarface," "Carlito's Way," "The Untouchables," or "Femme Fatale," he is undeniably a director with extraordinary talent.

While watching, I've seen many of the different camera angles, zoom-in shots, movement, and other technical stuff used to film this, and realized that this is a very "Brian De Palma" film. Having watched ten of his movies, I can easily pick up his directing style. His style brings a unique touch to this film.

Despite the fact that I enjoyed this flick, I did have many problems with it. The most notable issue is how predictable everything was. I do this real moron thing called thinking. I probably should have turned my brain off while watching because I somehow managed to predict everything, which left me unsatisfied. Whenever I watch movies, I like everything to be presented to me as the story flows, not when it begins in an obvious way. I understand the movie wanted to twists, but they were to obvious.

I believe the issue was that Brian De Palma attempted to blend multiple genres simultaneously. In addition to being an action film, it also incorporates elements of mystery, drama, and Hitchcockian thriller. The Hitchcock influence is expected, considering De Palma's other works, as he is heavily inspired by Hitchcock's films.

The rest of the movie, like all the action bits, is what makes this shine. It's just Tom Cruise doing what he does best in literally every action movie he's in. He makes this all about him. All of the stunts are done by him, which is pretty rare for actors to do their own stunts, especially in big-budget action films that require a lot of precision and skill. Props to Tom Cruise and his dexterity.

By the end, I learned that this movie is based on the TV show from 1966 to 1973. This got me curious to see how accurate this movie was. And, to nobody's surprise, it wasn't. The show is a team-building show, while the movie is just a Tom Cruise movie with the name from the show slapped onto the title. Curise said he was a fan of the original show, so he bought the rights to the show. But that never made sense to me. If he loved the original show, why wasn't the movie true to it? That's a mystery to me.

This first film in the series is a solid entry. I'm unsure about the quality of the sequels, but I do know that the third one was not good. I might watch the first three films at most, but I’m not certain if I'll go beyond that. If I do decide to watch them, you’ll likely see reviews from me.

Rating: 3/4 stars.
